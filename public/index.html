Getting started

Signing in

The admin makes your account and gives you a temporary password. The first time you sign in you'll choose your own. To change it later, tap More at the bottom right, then Account (on a computer, Account is at the top right). Want a different name shown (on shared loads, box photos and the greeting on Home)? Ask the admin; they can change it with Edit name on the Admin page.

Colors

To use metric, go to More → Account → Units and tap Metric. Distances then show in metres, velocities in m/s, bullet drop, drift and group sizes in centimetres, cartridge sizes (COAL, case and bullet length, twist, barrel) in millimetres, energy in joules, temperatures in °C, wind in km/h, altitude in metres and bulk powder in kilograms. Bullet weights and powder charges stay in grains. Nothing you've saved changes, only how it's shown, so you can switch back and forth. The setting follows your account to your other devices, and the Excel and PDF exports and printed targets use it too. In metric, the range table and dope card step in whole metres, the wind brackets are 10, 20 and 30 km/h, and a zero typed in metres stays that many metres. A zero you set in yards shows its metric equivalent (100 yd shows as 91 m). The help on this page uses imperial units.

Pick a color theme under More → Account → Theme: Range (the original), Olive Drab, Flat Dark Earth, Blued Steel, Blaze Orange or Red Night. Each one can follow your phone's light/dark setting or stay light or dark. Red Night is always dark red-on-black, so it won't wreck your night vision. Your choice follows your account to your other devices.

Getting around

Range Book opens on Home: buttons to start a range day or a new load, anything that needs attention (components running low, a barrel near the end of its life, a gun due for cleaning), your rounds fired this month and year, your recent range trips, the reloading loads you've worked on lately, and each gun's round count. Tap Range Book at the top to get back to it. On a phone the sections are along the bottom: Home, Shooting, Reloading, Guns & Ammo and More (How to Use, Admin, Account and Sign out). On a computer they're across the top. At the top of Reloading, Loads | Components switches between your recipes and your components; at the top of Shooting, Loads | Printable targets does the same. When you open a load or gun, ‹ Loads or ‹ Guns above its name takes you back to the list. On a phone or tablet you can also swipe in from the left edge of the screen to go back up a level (from a range trip to the load's trips, from a load to the list, from a gun to your guns), and the phone's own Back button does the same.

The Range log (the Range log button on Home, or Shooting → Range log) lists every day you've shot, newest first and grouped by month: your range trips, plus each day you shot groups for a Reloading load (one entry per load per day, with the number of groups, the charges, rounds and the best group). At the top are this year's days at the range, rounds, trips and load-development days, and your best group of the year. Tap Range trips or Load development to show just one kind, pick a gun to see only its days, or search by place, load or date. Tap an entry to open that trip, or that load's groups.

Start a range day on Home asks which gun you're shooting, then what you're shooting through it: one of your Shooting loads, or ammunition from your inventory (it makes the Shooting load for that gun the first time). It opens a new range trip dated today and, if your phone shares its location, fills in the temperature and wind. You can also start one from a gun's page.

Tap a card's title (Point-blank range, Range table, Groups, Photos, the sections of this How to Use page and so on) to fold it out of the way; tap it again to open it. Range Book remembers which cards you closed on that phone or computer. Long lists (loads, guns, range trips, components) get a Search box at the top: type part of a name, cartridge, place or date. When you delete something (a load, gun, trip, photo, group, component or ammunition), a note at the bottom says so with an Undo button for a few seconds. If you close Range Book and come back within 12 hours, it opens where you left off.

Add it to your iPhone home screen

Open this page in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. It gets its own icon and opens full screen.

When the server has a new version, the app tells you with a banner that floats at the bottom of the screen (just above the tabs on a phone), so you see it wherever you are on the page: tap Reload now to switch to it (you land back where you were). It checks when the app opens, when you switch back to Range Book and every minute while it's open, so there's no need to close the app.

Changed something on another phone or computer? In the home-screen app, scroll to the very top of the page and pull down until it says Release to refresh, then let go. Anything you were still typing is saved first, then the app reloads with the newest data and puts you back where you were. (In a browser tab, use the browser's own refresh.)

Where your data lives

Everything saves to your account automatically, so it follows you to any phone or computer you sign in on.

No signal at the range

Open Range Book once on your phone through its https:// address while you have a signal. After that it opens even with no signal, showing your loads and photos as of the last time it synced. Anything you change or add (shots, groups, photos, measurements) is kept on the phone, and a yellow Offline tag at the top shows how many changes are waiting. They upload by themselves when you get a signal again. Don't sign out while changes are waiting, or they're lost.

Guest mode

If the admin has turned it on, the sign-in page has a Try it as a guest button. Guests can use everything, but nothing is saved: it's all gone when the page is closed or reloaded.

Shooting

The Shooting tab is the trajectory calculator plus your range trips and printable targets.

Pick or make a load

The Shooting tab (the trajectory calculator and your range trips) opens to a list of your loads, grouped by cartridge and sorted by name (long-range loads are tagged). Set a load's Cartridge at the top of its inputs to move it to that group. Tap one to open it, and tap ‹ Loads to go back. To add your own, tap + New load (chart to 400 yd) or + New long-range load (chart to 1,000 yd, table to 2,000 yd). It starts as a copy of the load you were looking at. Your loads are saved to your account. To remove a load, open it and tap Delete load at the very bottom (Undo brings it back). That works for the example loads Range Book starts with too; those also have Reset to defaults if you've changed one and want the original back.

A new Range Book also comes with a SAMPLE · 6.5 Creedmoor load on Shooting and, for the first account, a matching worked-up ladder on Reloading. They're made up to show how things fit together, and they're marked with a warning: don't load or shoot anything from them without checking a current load manual. Delete them whenever you like.

Fill in the fields

  • Rifle: pick one from your Guns and Ammo tab and its sight height fills in. Loads sent over from Reloading come with the gun already picked. If you change the gun's sight height on the Guns and Ammo tab, the calculator follows unless you've typed in your own.
  • Bullet weight: from the ammo box.
  • Muzzle velocity: the box number comes from a test barrel and is often 50–150 fps faster than your gun. A chronograph reading is best.
  • Velocity measured at and Temp sensitivity (optional): powder burns faster when it's warm, so a load is faster in summer than in winter. Enter the temperature your velocity was measured at and how many fps it changes for every 10°F (often 5–20 fps), and the calculator adjusts the velocity for the temperature on the Conditions card. Under the load's Range trips, a Velocity and temperature chart can work out the sensitivity from your own trips (it needs trips with an average velocity and temperatures at least 10°F apart); tap Use … in the calculator to fill it in. When you use a trip's average as the muzzle velocity, its temperature is saved as "measured at".
  • Ballistic coefficient (G1): on the box or the bullet maker's website. Use the G1 number, not G7.
  • Bullet diameter: e.g. .224, .243, .277, .308, .338, .357. Used for sectional density and the form-factor check below the inputs. The path itself comes from the BC.
  • Zeros: the four boxes at the bottom of the Bullet path card, under the chart. Type up to four distances to compare, each with its own color. Leave one blank to hide it.
  • Scope zero: tap the circle in the zero box your scope is actually set to (that box gets a highlighted border). The range table's MOA and MIL columns and the dope card are based on it.
  • Conditions (its own card, under Inputs): hot weather and high altitude make bullets drop less. For the wind, enter its speed and the o'clock it comes from (12 is straight at you, 3 from the right, 9 from the left); the calculator works out how much of it pushes the bullet sideways and which way to hold. Head and tail winds barely matter and aren't counted.
  • Shot angle: for shooting up or down hill, enter the angle (either way, it's the same). Only part of the drop counts at an angle, so you hold or dial less than on level ground; the table, chart and dope card all use it. Leave it blank for level shots.
  • Range table columns: under the range table, tick Adjust (MOA), Adjust (MIL) and Wind to choose which of those columns to show (Wind includes the wind brackets and spin drift). Range, velocity, energy, time and the path are always there. Your choice is remembered on that phone or computer.
  • Wind brackets: turn this on to add 5, 10 and 15 mph full-value wind holds to the range table and dope card (and Field mode), so you can hold for whatever the wind is doing without changing the inputs.

Reading the results

  • Near, Far and Top: shown in each zero box. Where each path rises through 0 (near) and comes back down through it (far).
  • Highest point: how far above your line of sight each path climbs, and at what range. It's listed as Top in each zero box under the chart. Handy for knowing how high you'll hit at mid ranges, or for a point-blank zero.
  • Path (in): inches above (+) or below (−) your line of sight, for the zero you picked with the circle in its zero box (pick a different one and the column follows). Negative means hold or dial up.
  • Adjust (MOA / MIL): how far to dial up from the zero your scope is set to.
  • Wind: drift for a full-value crosswind. Hold or dial into the wind.

Point-blank range

The Point-blank range card (the Point-blank link at the top) works out the best zero for holding dead on. Enter the size of what you're shooting at, or tap 4" varmint, 6" deer or 8" elk. It finds the zero that keeps the bullet within half that size above or below your line of sight for as long as possible, and shows the near and far zero for that path (zero at either one; the far one is more forgiving), where the bullet stays on target (from how close to how far), the maximum point-blank range, and the highest point. On the chart it draws the target band as a shaded box, that zero's path as a dashed purple line with purple diamonds at its near and far zeros, and a line at the maximum point-blank range. Use the far zero or Use the near zero adds it to your zeros (or replaces zero 4 if they're all full). It uses the load, gun and conditions you've entered, and doesn't include wind.

G1 or G7

Pick the BC type that matches the number you have. Many long-range bullets list a G7 number (around 0.2–0.35); it's usually more accurate past 600 yd. The Reloading recipe has the same choice and sends it over with Use in calculator.

Stability and spin drift

Enter the barrel twist (1 turn in so many inches; it fills in from your gun) and the bullet length (measure one with calipers, tip to base). You then see the stability number (SG: 1.4 or more is good, under 1.0 the bullet won't fly straight) and a spin drift column. With a normal right-hand twist the bullet drifts right; add that to your wind hold.

True up the velocity

Once you've shot at long range, enter the distance and the dial that actually hit under True up velocity. It works out the muzzle velocity that matches your real drop; tap Use and the table and dope card will line up with what you saw. Use a distance well past your zero and a good group.

Weather where you are

On the Conditions card, Use the weather where I am fills in the temperature and the altitude from today's air pressure where you're standing. It needs a signal and the https:// (Cloudflare) address, since phones only share location with secure sites. Set the crosswind yourself, since it depends on which way you're shooting.

Print a dope card

Below the range table, Dope card makes a small card for your stock or range bag. Choose the first and last distance, how often, and whether your scope clicks in MOA or MIL. It uses the zero your scope is set to, the conditions and the crosswind you entered. Tap Print dope card to print just the card (about 3½ in wide), or save it as a PDF from the print screen. On an iPhone, pinch out on the print preview to save or share it.

Field mode fills the screen with just the distance, how much to dial up and the wind hold, in big numbers you can read at the bench. A− and A+ change the size, Bright switches to black on white for full sun, and the screen stays on while it's open (on phones that allow it). Tap ← Back or swipe in from the left to close it.

The inputs fold up on a phone

On a phone, when you open a load the Inputs card folds into a one-line summary (bullet, velocity, BC, gun, zero, weather) so the results are near the top. Tap it, or the Inputs link, to open it and change anything. Every other card on a load (Point-blank range, Bullet path, Range table, Dope card, Range trips and the rest) folds up too: tap its title to close or open it. The app remembers which ones you closed on that phone or computer, and the links at the top open a closed card for you.

Range trips

Every load in the calculator has a Range trips log at the bottom (tap Range trips in the row of links at the top to jump there). Tap + New range trip and fill in what you like: date, where, distance, rounds fired, temperature, wind, average velocity and SD, and notes. Use the weather where I am fills in the temperature and wind. Import chronograph file reads the same RangeCraft, Garmin Xero, MagnetoSpeed and LabRadar files as Reloading; each string is kept with its shots, average, SD and ES, and the trip's average velocity and SD are worked out from all of them (tap Remove on a string you don't want). Add target photos and tap one to measure its groups, the same way as in Reloading; printed Range Book targets are read by themselves. Print a target for this trip opens a printable target with the trip's load, date, distance and gun already filled in at the top. The trip list shows the best group from each trip and how many rounds you've logged. If you enter an average velocity, a button lets you make it this load's muzzle velocity. Print trip report at the bottom of a trip opens a PDF with the details, notes, chronograph strings and target photos with the groups drawn on, ready to print or save. You don't need a reloading load for any of this, so it works just as well for factory ammo. Your Reloading photos and data stay where they are. In Chronograph, each string shows its shots in numbered boxes, the same as a group in Reloading. Type over a shot to fix it, tap + Shot to add one, or tap − Shot, tap the shots you want gone (a bad reading, an unused box) and tap Delete. The average, SD and ES update, and Undo puts them back. To type velocities in yourself, tap + Type in shots for a new string with 5 boxes.

Zoom the chart

Pinch to zoom and drag with one finger to move around. A sideways pinch stretches the range, an up-and-down pinch stretches the inches. Double-tap the chart or tap Reset view to go back. On a computer, use the zoom buttons or pinch on a trackpad. Declutter hides the near and far yardage labels on the line of sight (the colored diamonds stay, and the numbers are in the zero boxes); tap it again to bring them back. The normal chart stretches the up-and-down direction a lot so you can read the drop in inches. True scale draws it at its real proportions, the same scale up and down as across, so you see how flat the bullet really flies; a 1,000 yd shot is 36,000 inches long, so even several feet of drop is a gentle curve. The up-and-down numbers switch to feet when they get big. Zooming in keeps the bullet centered and the proportions true. Tap it again to go back. Both buttons are highlighted while they're on and are remembered on this device. Drag the small bar under the chart up or down to make the chart shorter or taller (double-tap the bar to go back to the normal height). The picture doesn't stretch or squash: a taller chart shows more inches of drop and a shorter one shows fewer, so drag on a short chart to move up and down. The height is remembered on this device.

Printable targets

Under Shooting → Printable targets (or the Printable targets button on Home) are targets made for Range Book. On regular Letter (8.5 × 11 in) paper: 5 aim points, a 9-point ladder grid, and 1 big aim point. On Tabloid / Ledger (11 × 17 in) paper: a 12-point ladder grid, 6 big aim boxes, and 1 big aim point with 1- to 4-inch rings for zeroing or long range. Most home printers can't print 11 × 17, but a print shop or office store can; ask for Actual size on Tabloid / Ledger paper. The app tells the two sizes apart on its own when you measure a photo. The background is a grid of 1-inch squares, and each aim point (a light red circle with a solid black center dot) sits in the middle of a square, so you can count inches by eye. The page has a 1-inch black square in each corner (the top-left one is hollow so the app knows which way is up). Tap Open PDF to print and print at Actual size (100%); the 1-inch line at the bottom should measure exactly 1 inch.

Write the charge for each aim point on its line before you shoot. Afterwards, photograph the whole page with all four squares showing, add the photo to the load, and tap Measure groups. It finds the page, sets the scale, corrects for an angled photo, and marks the holes around every aim point by itself. Aim points with no holes are left out. Check each group and pick its charge, then Save. If it doesn't find the page, tap Read printed target after a better photo, or measure by hand.

Measure sight height (height over bore)

The distance from the center of the bore to the center of the scope. It matters most at short range.

  1. Unload the gun and confirm it is clear.
  2. Find your scope tube size: 1 in, 30 mm (1.18 in), or 34 mm (1.34 in).
  3. Just in front of the receiver, measure the barrel diameter with calipers.
  4. At that same spot, measure from the top of the scope tube to the bottom of the barrel.
  5. Sight height = that measurement − ½ tube diameter − ½ barrel diameter.

3.70 in total, 30 mm tube, 0.75 in barrel → 3.70 − 0.59 − 0.375 = 2.74 in

Iron sights: measure from the top of the front sight post to the bottom of the barrel there, then subtract ½ the barrel diameter. Typical values: bolt or lever gun with low rings ≈ 1.5 in, medium/high rings ≈ 1.75–2.0 in, AR with optic ≈ 2.5–2.8 in.

Reloading

Add a load

  1. On the Reloading tab, tap + New load. Enter the cartridge, pick the gun, and pick the bullet, powder, primer and brass from your Components (anything not saved yet can be typed into the recipe afterward). Add the Max charge from your load manual, then tap Create and plan a ladder to go straight to the ladder planner, or Create.
  2. Any group at the max charge gets an At max tag, and any group over it gets a red Over max warning.
  3. Pick the Rifle you shot it in (add guns on the Guns & Ammo tab first).
  4. Under Ladder tests, tap + Add group in a ladder for each charge weight you test and enter the charge (or let the ladder planner make them all).
  5. Type each shot's FPS. Press Enter on the last box (or tap + Shot) to add another shot. Average, SD, extreme spread and energy fill in as you type. Groups start with 5 boxes. To get rid of some, say if you only fired 4 or a reading is bad, tap − Shot, tap each shot you want gone (they turn red and crossed out), then tap Delete. The shots after them move up, and Undo puts them back. A box left blank isn't counted either. Range trips work the same way.
  6. Enter each group's size in Group size (center to center of the two widest holes), or measure it from a target photo (below). The Distance is entered once at the top of the ladder. The MOA is worked out for you, so groups shot at different distances can be compared.

Reloading notes

Reloading → Notes is for anything that isn't about one load: die and press settings, what to order, ideas to try, a powder that's back in stock. Tap + New note, give it a title if you like (otherwise its first line is the title) and type. It saves as you go. Pin a note to keep it at the top, search them from the box above the list, and delete one from the bottom of the note (with an Undo). Notes are private and are included when you export your data.

Develop and Load

Each load has two views, switched at the top. Develop is for working it up: the recipe, ladder tests, ladder planner, summary, charts and target photos. Load is the finished load as a load sheet you can't edit: the charge and powder, bullet, primer, brass, trim length, COAL, bullet diameter, your manual's max and the gun, with the velocity and best group at that charge, Use in calculator, and Loaded rounds for logging batches. To change anything on it, go back to Develop.

When your groups show what works, tap This is my load next to that charge in the Summary, or use Create the load at the bottom of Develop. That charge becomes the working charge: the load opens on Load from then on, and the charge is filled in for you when you log a batch, add the load to your ammo, work out the cost per round or send it to the calculator. To pick another, use Ready to load it? on Develop again.

Turn on Locked (on Develop) once a load is done. That locks the whole Develop page (recipe, groups, photos, sharing) except Ready to load it?, which moves to the top of the page so you can still pick a different working charge. A locked load can't be deleted, updating a component under Components leaves it alone, and you can still log batches on Load. Turn Locked off to make changes.

In the recipe, a bullet, powder, primer or brass picked from Components shows as one tinted line with its details. The bullet's weight, length, diameter and BC come from the saved bullet, so you change them in one place (under Components, then tap Update there). Tap Change to pick a different one or type it in, and Done to fold it back to one line.

Finding your way around a load

The buttons under a load's name (Recipe, Summary, Charts, Targets, Ladder tests) jump straight to that part of the page. Once a load has velocities, its recipe folds up into a one-line summary; tap Recipe to open it again.

Inventory

Every bullet, powder and primer under Reloading → Components has an On hand amount (count for bullets and primers, pounds for powder) and a Warn when level. On a reloading load, the Loaded rounds section (the Loaded link at the top) logs each batch you load: how many rounds, the charge, the brass lot and the date. Logging it takes the bullets, primers and powder (rounds × charge) out of inventory (a ladder test does the same with Take components out of inventory), as long as the recipe uses saved components with an amount filled in. Remove on a batch puts them back. Anything that drops below its warning level shows as Low on the Components list and on Home.

Cost per round

Each component also has a Cost section: what you paid and for how many (a box of 100 bullets, an 8 lb jug of powder, a brick of 1,000 primers). For brass, the price is spread over how many times you expect to load each case (5 if you leave it blank). The Loaded rounds section on a load then shows what each round costs, with the bullet, powder (at the charge you enter), primer and brass broken out, and each batch you log keeps its total cost.

Brass lots

Under Reloading → Components → Brass, add a lot for each batch of cases you keep together. Enter how many cases, how many times they'd been loaded before Range Book, and when you last annealed and trimmed them (Annealed today and Trimmed today fill in the date). Each batch you log with that lot adds one to its load count. The old 5.56 → 300 Blackout headstamp notes are folded up at the bottom of the Brass list; tap them to open.

Components: bullets, powders and primers

Reloading → Components keeps the components you use: bullets (maker, name, the caliber as sold like 38 Special or 30 cal, the actual diameter like .357 used for the math, weight, BC, BC type, length), powders, primers and brass lots. Tap + Add to enter one, or Add the … from my loads to fill the list from recipes you've already entered. For powders, Add a powder from the list has about 150 popular ones from Hodgdon, IMR, Winchester, Alliant, Accurate, Ramshot, Vihtavuori and Norma, grouped by maker, with a search box at the top (type a name, maker or kind, like "h4350" or "alliant ball"); picking one fills in the maker, name and kind (ball, extruded or flake) for you. The same names are suggested as you type a powder into a recipe, and a listed name fills in its maker. If you change a saved bullet, powder or primer later (a corrected BC, a new name), its Loads card marks the loads that still have the old details; tap Update to copy the new details into them, along with any reloaded ammo made from them. A recipe that's out of date also shows Update this recipe under that component. Inside a load, the Components › button at the right end of the shortcut bar takes you straight to Components, with a ‹ Back to button to return to that load. The recipe's Brass can be picked from your saved brass lots the same way; it fills in the brass and its case length (add a Case length to the lot under Components), and makes that lot the default when you log a batch. COAL stays with each load, since it depends on the bullet and seating depth.

In a recipe, pick a saved component from the shaded box above the Bullet, Primer or Powder fields; it fills in the details for you (for a bullet: weight, BC, length and diameter too). You can still type everything by hand. If you type one that isn't saved yet, a Save to Components button appears so you can keep it for next time. Cartridges you type are remembered too, and the cartridge and maker boxes suggest what you've used before as you type.

Ladder tests

A load's groups live in Ladder tests. Each ladder (Ladder 1, Ladder 2 and so on) is one batch you loaded and shot, kept separate from the others. At the top of each ladder are its Loaded date, Tested date, Temperature (°F) and Distance, entered once for the whole ladder. Each ladder can have its own distance, and every group in it uses that distance for its MOA. Under them are its groups, one per charge, then its target photos. Inside a ladder, + Add group adds another charge, and Import chronograph file as new group brings in velocities as new groups in that ladder. Delete ladder removes the ladder and its groups (with an Undo). Tap a ladder's title to fold it down to its title and summary line, and tap again to open it; each phone or computer remembers which ones you folded.

Once you've loaded a ladder, tap Take components out of inventory under its dates. It works like logging a batch: one round for each shot box at each charge, so it takes that many bullets and primers, plus the powder for every charge, out of your Components. Pick the Brass lot you used and that lot's load count goes up by one. The ladder then shows what it took. Put them back undoes it, say if you change the ladder and want to log it again. Ladder rounds aren't added to your ammo inventory, since you shoot them for the test. Deleting a ladder doesn't change your inventory, so tap Put them back first if you never loaded it.

To start another ladder, tap Plan a new ladder at the bottom of Ladder tests, or + Empty ladder to set up the groups yourself. Your first ladder stays as it was. When a load has more than one ladder, the Summary and Velocity by charge show one ladder at a time (the newest to start); pick another, or All ladders, from Showing at the top of them. Tap Combine matching charges to see each charge once, with every shot at that charge from all your ladders pooled: the average velocity, SD and ES come from all of them, and the group is the average of the groups you measured at that charge. Your ladders and their shots aren't changed; tap it again to go back.

Plan a ladder test

Under Ladder tests, tap Plan a new ladder. Enter the first charge, the step and the last charge (it starts with your manual's max) plus how many shots per charge. It shows every charge first, flagging any over max, then Create ladder makes a new ladder with a group for each charge, ready for your chronograph import. (On a brand-new load, it fills the empty Ladder 1 instead of adding a second one.)

The planner has a Distance, a Loaded date (today to start) and a Tested date you can leave blank until you shoot. They go at the top of the new ladder, where you can change them later.

To label the box of ladder rounds, tap Print a label in the planner (or Ladder label in a ladder once its groups exist; it uses that ladder's charges and dates). One 3.5 × 2 in label holds the recipe and every charge, numbered in order with a box to tick as you load each one, plus shots per charge, distance, the loaded date and the tested date (or a blank line to write it on).

Velocity by charge

Once two or more groups have charges and shots, the Velocity by charge chart plots average speed for each charge, with a bar showing ± one SD. Look for a flat spot where adding powder barely changes the speed: that's usually the most forgiving charge. Under the chart, the fps gained per grain between each step is listed. The dashed red line is your max charge. Groups with a size also get a purple diamond showing their MOA on the right-hand scale, so you can see which charge shot tightest.

Import from your chronograph

Set up a group for each charge weight in the ladder first. Then tap Import chronograph file on the ladder's first group and pick the export from your RangeCraft (.xlsx), Garmin Xero (ShotView .csv or .xlsx) MagnetoSpeed (LOG.CSV from the V3's memory card, or the .csv from the XFR app) or LabRadar (the SR0001 Report.csv files from its SD card; pick several at once and each series fills its own group, SR0001 first). Each string fills the next empty group in that ladder in the order you shot them. If the ladder has no tested date yet, it takes the date from the file when the file has one (MagnetoSpeed's memory-card file doesn't, so set that date yourself). Check that each string landed on the right charge weight. If the chronograph caught a bad reading (a miss or an echo), tap − Shot, tap that shot and tap Delete.

Target photos

Each target photo belongs to the ladder you shot it with. Tap + Photo in that ladder (or next to the ladder's name on the Targets card) to take a picture or choose one. The Targets card shows the photos under each ladder, and each ladder shows its own photos too. Tap a photo to see it full size, add a caption, delete it, or move it to another ladder with Shot with. When you measure groups on a photo, you pick from that ladder's charges. Photos added before ladders existed are linked for you when the app can tell which ladder they go with; open any that say Pick a ladder and choose one.

Measure groups from a photo

One photo can hold several groups, each with its own aim point and charge weight.

  1. Tap a target photo, then Measure groups.
  2. Scale (once per photo): tap two points a known distance apart, such as two grid lines 1 inch apart, and enter that distance in Scale (in). Check Distance (yd) too.
  3. Aim: tap the first group's aim point.
  4. Box: drag a box around that group's shots. It tries to mark the holes inside. Entering Shots fired first helps; it fills in from the charge's chronograph shots when it can.
  5. Shots: check every mark. Tap a wrong one to remove it, tap a missed hole to add it, drag a mark to move it. Holes that touch usually need adding by hand. Pinch to zoom.
  6. Under This group is, pick the charge weight it goes with.
  7. Tap + Group for the next group and repeat from the aim point. Tap a group's chip (or any of its marks) to go back to it.
  8. Tap Save. Each matched charge gets its group size and distance, and the group MOA shows on the Velocity by charge chart.

Group size is center to center of the two farthest shots, the same as calipers across the outside edges minus one bullet width.

Use a load in the calculator

In a load's Summary, tap Use in calculator on a group. It makes a new trajectory load with that bullet weight, average velocity, the ballistic coefficient from the recipe, and the sight height of the gun you picked. Anything missing, fill in on the Shooting tab.

Other tools

  • Duplicate recipe copies a load without its shots, batches or working charge, handy for a retest. Pick a different powder or bullet there to start working up a variation.
  • Your brass headstamp ratings are under Reloading → Components → Brass, folded up at the bottom.
  • Shot velocities are whole numbers. A decimal you type is rounded when you leave the box.

Share a load

Open the load and tick people under Sharing. They can see the load, its velocities and photos but can't change anything. Untick to stop sharing. Loads shared with you appear under Shared with me; use Duplicate recipe to make your own editable copy.

Export your data

Tap More → Account (or Account at the top on a computer), then Download Excel or Download PDF. The PDF includes your target photos.

Guns & Ammo

Guns

The Guns & Ammo tab lists your guns under Guns. Tap + Add gun or tap a gun to open it, then fill in its serial number, barrel, twist rate, sight height and notes. Under Photos, tap + Add photos to keep pictures of the gun; the first one shows in your list of guns. Gun photos and serial numbers are private to you, and the serial numbers are included when you export your data, which is handy for insurance. To export just your guns, tap Export Excel or Export PDF at the top of the Guns list: the spreadsheet has one row per gun (serial number, barrel, twist, round count, barrel life, cleaning), and the PDF has a summary table, then a page for each gun with its details, notes and photos. Each gun's page also lists the loads and range trips that use it; tap one to jump to it. When a load has a gun picked, Use in calculator also brings that gun's sight height across.

Round count and barrel life

Each gun's page has a Round count. It adds the rounds from range trips on Shooting loads that use that gun, the shots in your load-development groups on Reloading loads that use it, and the Rounds before Range Book you enter. Fill in Barrel life to see a bar of how much is used (it turns red at 80%, and Home warns you). Set Clean every and tap Cleaned today after cleaning to see the rounds since then; Home tells you when it's due. When you fit a new barrel, set Barrel fitted on to that date and Rounds before Range Book to 0.

Ammunition

Guns & Ammo → Ammunition keeps the ammo you shoot, in two lists: Reloaded ammo and Factory ammo. Each one counts the rounds you have on hand (loaded or bought, minus what your range trips fire) and can warn you when you're running low.

Reloaded ammo

Each batch in the batch history has a Label button (and one appears right after you log a batch): it shows a label with the load name, bullet, powder and charge, primer, brass lot, COAL, velocity, date and round count. Pick how many copies and tap Print labels; they print 3.5 × 2 in (business-card size), ten to a page, on plain paper or Avery 5371/5376-style card stock or labels.

To compare loads, tap Compare loads at the top of the Reloading list and pick up to three. A table shows the components, the working charge (or the last one used) with its velocity, SD, ES and group, the best group and lowest SD at any charge, cost per round and rounds loaded. The lowest SD, ES, group size and cost are highlighted. Tap a load's name to open it.

To keep count of rounds you've loaded, open a recipe on the Reloading tab, switch to Load and log a batch in Loaded rounds. The first batch at a charge adds the load to your ammo inventory, copying the bullet, BC, powder, primer and brass from the recipe and the velocity from your groups at that charge. Each later batch at the same charge adds to the count, removing a batch takes it back off, and range trips with it count it down. The card shows how many you have on hand, and the batch history folds away underneath. You can also start one from Ammunition → + Reloaded ammo: pick a recipe, or choose None: I'll pick the parts myself, then pick the bullet, powder, primer and brass from your Components and fill in the charge. Each part shows its details underneath (weight, BC, diameter, kind, case length, on hand, cost); tap Edit to change them on the Components page, then ‹ Back to to return. + Create new at the top of each list makes a new component and opens it so you can fill it in. An entry can also be linked to a recipe later with Fill in from this recipe. Once you've loaded it, turn on Locked at the top so the recipe (name, cartridge, bullet, powder, charge, primer, brass and COAL) can't be changed by accident, not even by an Update from Components; the rounds, velocity and notes stay editable. Duplicate at the bottom makes an unlocked copy (with no rounds yet) to start a new one from. The cost per round comes from the prices on your components, or type it in. Use in calculator makes a Shooting load from it, just like factory ammo.

Factory ammo

On Guns & Ammo → Ammunition, tap a caliber under Browse by caliber (they're grouped into Rifle, Handgun and Rimfire) to see what's available, or type in the search box. There are about 155 popular loads in 56 cartridges, from 17 HMR to 50 BMG (Hornady, Federal, Sig Sauer, Remington, CCI, Speer, Nosler and Barnes; rifle, rimfire and handgun) with the bullet, BC and box velocity already filled in from the maker's published data (Nosler and Barnes velocities come from gun-shop listings instead, with the BC from the makers' bullet data); tap one to add it to your list. Not listed? Tap + Enter factory ammo yourself and fill in the maker, cartridge, bullet, weight, BC, the velocity on the box (and your measured velocity if you've chronographed it), plus rounds bought, price and lot number. Use in calculator (pick the gun first if you like) makes a load on the Shooting tab with those numbers. You can also pick the ammo in a Shooting load's Ammunition box. Tap it to open a short list: your reloaded ammo, your factory ammo, and the catalog loads for that load's cartridge. Type in its search box (a maker, powder, bullet weight or cartridge) to search everything, including the whole catalog. Picking a catalog load fills in the numbers and adds it to your Ammunition for you. The range trips you log on that load count down your rounds on hand (bought minus fired), and + Bought more adds a new box. Set Warn when fewer than and Home tells you when you're running low. If your range trips have average velocities, it offers to use their average as your measured velocity.

Sharing factory loads

Entered a factory load that isn't in the catalog? Open it and tick Share this load with everyone on this Range Book. It shares the maker, product, cartridge, bullet, weight, BC, diameter, box velocity and its box photo; your rounds, price, lot, measured velocity and notes stay private. Changes you make later update the shared copy, and unticking it (or deleting the ammo) removes it. Shared loads show up in their caliber's box under Browse by caliber and in searches, shaded and marked SHARED with the person's name so you can tell them from the maker's data; tap one to add it to their own list. You can remove your own shared loads there, and admins can remove any of them. If someone's account is deleted, the loads they shared (and their box photos) stay for everyone, listed as shared by a former member.

Box photos

Open any factory ammo and tap + Add a photo of the box. A quick phone snap is fine; it's shrunk before it's saved. If the ammo came from the catalog, the photo is shared with everyone on this Range Book, so the next person can tell at a glance it's the right box, and it shows next to that load in the catalog. Photos of ammo you entered yourself stay private. You can delete your own photos; admins can delete any shared photo that doesn't match (there's a list of them all on the Admin page).

The fine print

Shooting is dangerous.

Guns are loud, fast and unforgiving. Gunpowder sitting in your garage is too. You knew that, but the lawyers in my head insisted.

This is a notebook, not a load manual.

Range Book is a log and a calculator built by a guy who likes to shoot. It is not a load manual, a gunsmith, or your mom. Every charge weight in here is just a number somebody typed, and somebody makes typos. That includes the max-charge warnings, the ladder test planner and anything someone shared with you. Check every load against a current published manual from the powder or bullet maker before it goes in a case. Start low, work up, and watch for pressure signs. If your primers look like pancakes, stop.

The math is an estimate.

Trajectories, dope cards, true-up, spin drift, the weather and group measurements are all educated guesses. Confirm your dope on paper before you bet a shot or a hunt on it. Nothing here tells you whether a shot is safe; that's on you. Know your target and what's behind it.

Software breaks.

There may be bugs. Data may get lost. Back up anything you care about (Account → Download Excel).

The part where you agree.

You're responsible for your own guns, ammo, components and decisions. If you do something dumb, like blowing up a rifle, hurting yourself, hurting someone else, or putting a hole in something you didn't mean to, that's on you. It's not on the person who runs this site or anyone who helped build it. Range Book comes with no warranty of any kind, express or implied. If you can't live with that, close this page and go read a reloading manual. (You should probably do that anyway.)

Follow the four rules, wear eye and ear protection, and keep the powder away from the cigarette.
